When searching for a project idea for my Computer Graphics course, I was thinking about things that might actually interest me. Keep in mind, I’m completely new to this subject, a real noob one might say. Everything I’ve learned so far is brand new to me; I hadn't even heard of ray tracing before this course (as you might’ve guessed, I'm not a hardcore gamer).

However, what's fascinated me since diving into graphics are all the cool visual effects you see in games — things like god rays, bloom, and basically anything that makes a scene look atmospheric and pretty. So that’s where my project hunt began: I wanted to make something that makes something pretty.

At first, I started researching how to achieve god rays. That led me to volumetric rendering, which led me to clouds, which ultimately led me to rainbows. And honestly, what’s prettier than rainbows?

So, that’s the goal I set for myself: a complete noob trying to create a physically-based simulation of a rainbow, computer graphics style. Did I mention that physics has never been my strongest subject?

Now, one might think I’m a bit naive (full-send foolish) for trying to take on something like this. And I’d agree. But that's me — if someone tells me I can’t do something (even if that person is myself), I see that as a dare more than a warning. We’ll see how that works out this time.

With all that said, this blog will follow my journey — complete with posts about topics that might be common knowledge to some, but are completely new territory for me. I want to truly understand what I’m doing, not just go through the motions. Since I’m starting from almost zero, I’ll be digging into all the nitty-gritty details to squeeze as much learning as possible out of this project. So if you’re reading and thinking “Wait, doesn’t everyone already know this?” — maybe! But I don’t, and I’m documenting the whole mess anyway.
